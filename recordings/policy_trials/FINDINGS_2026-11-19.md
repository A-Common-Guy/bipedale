# Trial of 2026-11-19 morning (robot on 6f79976)

Read from the recording and the robot's launch log alone; the operator's
observations are not in here (add them as `notes.md` inside the run).

## policies_20261119-073801 (95 s of `rl_agile_step`, camera on)

The first trial with the camera running (`camera:=true`, `/terrain_profile`
at 22 Hz in the bag) and with `safety.oscillation_hold_s` 1.2. No wheel
filter. Driven to 1 m/s; 68 s of the 95 driving.

**Hops.** Eight requested (`/cmd_vel` `linear.y` 0.05) at 51.0, 54.5, 66.9,
68.8, 77.3, 92.1, 93.0 and 94.1 s. None tripped the oscillation watchdog,
which cut every hop the evening before. The controller's own reading
(`oscillation_leg_rad_s`, 50 Hz diagnostics) peaks at 1.95-2.33 rad/s per hop
and is beyond the 1.0 limit for 0.70-0.80 s after a single hop, as in
simulation (2.2-2.4 rad/s, 0.7-0.8 s). Hops less than 2 s apart add up: 1.1
to 1.7 s beyond the limit within 3 s (66.9 + 68.8, 92.1 + 93.0). That did not
trip the 1.2 s hold here, so the stretches were not continuous, but the
margin for hops in quick succession is thin. Pitch stays within -16 to +10
degrees through a hop.

**The end: a drive fault, not a fall.** At 94.6 s, half a second after the
third hop in 2 s at 1 m/s (pitch at +21 degrees), both wheel drives report
`axis error 0x40 flags=9` and the hardware goes down. The same code stopped
`left_wheel` 46 s into the previous run of the same session (before this
recording) and `left_wheel` and `right_motor_inner` on 2026-11-18, each time
with `rl_agile_step` hopping. The drives are at 31-36 C and report no error
until that moment; the largest measured wheel torque in the recording is
7.9 Nm, leg torque 13.7 Nm. What 0x40 with flags 9 means in the drive's
firmware is not decoded in this repository. Two drives failing in the same
cycle points at something they share, the supply during a landing, rather
than at one motor; that is a guess, not a measurement (the bag has no bus
voltage).

**Standing.** 0.07 rad/s of pitch at 12 Hz (typical 2 s window; quiet is
below 0.06, the evening before 0.12 without the camera).

**The profile looks too high.** Where known, 47 % of the samples are more
than 2 cm off the floor, the far half (0.8-1.35 m) reads 4 to 12 cm on
average over every 10 s of the run, and the clip (+25 cm) is reached
throughout. The node's floor fit reads pitch +1.5 degrees, roll +0.3, offset
-4 mm mid-run. The recording cannot tell walls and furniture ahead (which
read as their height from their face on) from a profile that rises with
distance because of the mount; 1.5 degrees is 3.5 cm at 1.35 m. Known points:
62-99 % per sample, lowest at 0.1-0.2 m ahead of the left wheel. A policy
that stops before anything taller than 6 cm will hesitate on such a profile:
check it standing on a flat, empty floor before judging the policy.

## Against the operator's observations (same day, all three recordings)

The operator reported: `rl_stand_up_v2` smoother but using much more floor
lying on the Pi's side (nose up); v6 much quieter; `rl_agile_step` an agile
policy that vibrates and does not react to the camera; the hop slows the
robot down before it leaves the floor.

**Stand-up, floor used** (axle travel from the wheel angles and the pitch,
from the first motion until 6 s later):

| Start | Robot, `rl_stand_up_v2` | Simulation, v2 | Simulation, `stand_up` |
|---|---|---|---|
| nose up (-0.73 to -0.79 rad) | 0.24, 0.26, 0.69, 1.23 m; upright after 1.3, 1.3, 2.8, 3.0 s | 0.24-0.27 m, 1.1-1.2 s | 0.7-1.8 m, 1.2-1.5 s |
| nose down (+0.67 rad) | 0.43 m, 0.4 s (one start) | 0.11-0.16 m, 0.6 s | 0.27-0.37 m, 0.6-0.9 s |

Two of the four nose-up starts match the simulation; the other two take more
than twice as long and three to five times the floor. The simulation does not
show it at floor frictions of 0.4, 0.6 or 1.0 (fitted plant, three seeds), so
friction alone is not the cause. No recording of the old `rl_stand_up` on
the robot exists to compare with.

**The hop.** Seven of the eight hops were asked for within 0.5 s of pushing
the stick from standstill, at 0.5-0.75 m/s and still accelerating, so they
do not show a hop from a steady run. The eighth (69.6 s, 1 m/s in reverse)
keeps 1 m/s through the hop. In every hop the legs fold from 0.18 to 0.09 m
within 0.2 s and stay below 0.13 m for about 0.7 s after landing; in the
0.1-0.2 s of the push the wheels turn backwards against the body. In
simulation at 1 m/s the speed dips from 0.87 to 0.72 m/s. A hop from a
steady run on the robot is not in these recordings.

**The camera.** A third of the profile samples have something taller than
7 cm within 0.3 m of the wheels, and standing still the profile reads
+3 and +10 cm at 1.05 and 1.35 m ahead of the right wheel (+1 and +4 cm
left). Only about 4 s of the recording is a steady forward command, too
little to compare the speed with and without something ahead (0.41 against
0.56 of the commanded speed, 16 against 47 samples). Whether the policy
reacts cannot be read from this recording; it needs a run on a flat, empty
floor, then one slow approach to a box and one to a 3 cm board.

**The vibration** is the 11.5-12 Hz pitch line of FINDINGS_2026-11-18
(0.12 rad/s without the camera, 0.07 with it; v6 0.03): the loop common to
both sides, which has no measured plant and no margin in training.
